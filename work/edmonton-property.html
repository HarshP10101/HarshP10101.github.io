01 Civic data / statistics

Edmonton Property Value Drivers

What changes when you look past the citywide median?

+2.52%median citywide change

Tools & sources

Python · pandas · SciPy · statsmodels

View project on GitHub ↗

01 / Context

Why it mattered

A single growth figure can hide very different stories across neighbourhoods and housing types. I used Edmonton’s open assessment data to compare changes from 2022 to 2024 and examine which observable property features were associated with value.

02 / Method

How I approached it

I cleaned roughly 1.27 million raw assessment records, retaining 1,065,705 after cleaning. I matched properties across years to compare change, used t-tests and ANOVA with Tukey HSD for group differences, and fit an ordinary least squares model to account for property type, lot size, age and location.

03 / Findings

What came out of it

The median assessed value rose 2.52% citywide. Stillwater gained about 18% while Downtown fell about 9%. Single-family homes rose 5.3% while apartments fell 4.2%. The estimated garage-associated gap shrank from $287K in the raw comparison to $116K after accounting for other features.

04 / Takeaway

What I learned

The comparison changed once I accounted for what else differed between properties. Public records omit interior features, and assessed values can diverge from sale prices. The regression describes associations.
